## Corrections and simplifications (stated honestly)

- **Mains as DC.** Heist 5's 220 V wing is solved as a DC network (Workbook does the same for its 220 V examples with Ohm's law). There is no AC, phase or earth modelling.
- **Ideal-ish sources.** Batteries have 0.02 Ω internal resistance and wires 0.01 Ω so a short gives a huge but finite current. This shifts readings by a few hundredths (e.g. 12 V across 6 Ω reads 1.99 A); devices accept currents within 2 % of their rating so the arithmetic answer works.
- **Lamps are fixed resistors.** Real filament resistance rises with temperature; the game keeps R constant (as Workbook's examples do).
- **Fuse model.** A fuse melts instantly when the current exceeds its rating (no time-current curve).
- **Window sensors** are modelled as closed contacts (0.05 Ω) that open with the window — a normally-closed magnetic contact, as in real alarm loops.

## Learning events

The game logs anonymous events to `localStorage["short-circuit-log"]` only: level id, action (cut, join, place item + terminals, remove, replace fuse, open window, steal, fail reason, win, hint id), timestamp, attempt number and whether a hint had been used in that level. First unaided decisions can be told apart from hinted or repeated ones by `attempt` and `hinted`. Nothing leaves the device.

## Note for Workbook (found during review)

The [ohms-law](https://science.workbook.co.il/grade-8/chapter-4/ohms-law) page contains the line "with 3 Ω the current is 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A"; for 12 V across 3 Ω it should be 12 ÷ 3 = 4 A. The game does not repeat this.
